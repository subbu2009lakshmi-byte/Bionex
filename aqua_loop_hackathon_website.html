01 / The challenge

Can everyday waste help care for plants?

Coconut coir and cotton textile scraps can be useful resources. At the same time, potted plants need regular, consistent watering. AQUA-LOOP explores a simple way to connect these two challenges.

🥥

Coir waste

Coconut husk fibres are a renewable material that can be repurposed instead of discarded.

👕

Textile scraps

Clean, absorbent cotton offcuts can be tested as a replaceable water-carrying wick.

🪴

Plant care

Small pots can dry out between watering. A passive wick system may help deliver water gradually.

02 / Our solution

Meet AQUA-LOOP

A removable coconut-coir mat paired with a replaceable cotton wick and a small water reservoir. The mat supports the pot; the wick is the part intended to transport water.

1

Coir base: Clean coir fibres are secured into a removable mat.

2

Textile wick: A strip of washed cotton fabric connects water to the potting soil.

3

Reservoir: A stable container stores water for the wick to draw from.

4

Modular design: The mat and wick can be removed, cleaned, replaced and tested separately.

The cotton wick carries water by capillary action. The coir mat is a support/base, not the main water-delivery mechanism.

03 / Science behind it

Capillary action

Water can move through narrow spaces in absorbent materials. In a wick, adhesion between water and fibres, together with cohesion between water molecules, helps water travel along the fabric.

🧵

Absorbent cotton

The cotton strip is the water pathway. Its performance depends on weave, cleanliness, width and contact with soil.

💧

Water movement

Water travels along the wick and enters the potting medium when the wick is properly positioned.

📏

Test, don't assume

Wick flow rate and soil moisture must be measured. Too much water can waterlog the plant.

04 / Build the prototype

Simple materials. Clear design.

Materials checklist

  • Clean coconut coir fibres
  • Washed cotton textile scraps
  • Small stable water container
  • Plant pot with drainage opening
  • Plant and potting soil
  • Jute thread, tray and measuring cup
Use clean, dry, mould-free fibres. Keep the reservoir stable and below or beside the pot so it cannot tip over. Adjust the wick if the soil becomes too wet.

05 / Test the idea

A 7-day prototype experiment

Compare similar plants and pots under the same light and room conditions. Record water added, reservoir level and soil moisture daily. Use real readings; do not claim water savings before the test.

What to compare

Water used

Measure the amount of water supplied to each pot over the same period.

Soil moisture

Use the same moisture meter and measurement location, or a consistent observation scale.

Plant condition

Record visible changes and check for excessive wetness, mould or standing water.

06 / Sustainability & novelty

A better use for discarded fibres

♻️

Waste reuse

Gives coconut coir and clean cotton scraps a second application.

🔁

Replaceable parts

Separate mat and wick parts can be tested and replaced individually.

🌍

Local materials

Uses accessible natural and recovered materials in a small-scale prototype.

Novelty note: Self-watering pots and capillary-wick systems already exist. AQUA-LOOP should not be described as the first self-watering product. Its proposed innovation is the modular combination of a removable coir support mat and a replaceable recovered-cotton wick. A prior-art search and comparative tests are needed before making any uniqueness claim.
